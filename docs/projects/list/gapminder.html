Overview

This project uses the Gapminder dataset — the same data that Hans Rosling used in his famous TED talks1 — to explore how the world has changed between 1952 and 2007. The dataset contains life expectancy, GDP per capita, and population for 142 countries across 12 five-year snapshots.

NoteQuestions this analysis answers
  1. How has global life expectancy changed over time?
  2. Is there still a strong link between wealth and longevity?
  3. Which continents have gained the most?

The Relationship Between Wealth and Life Expectancy

Show setup code 
import plotly.express as px
import pandas as pd

df = px.data.gapminder()
print(f"Dataset covers {df['country'].nunique()} countries across {df['year'].nunique()} years")
print(f"Years: {df['year'].min()} to {df['year'].max()}")
Dataset covers 142 countries across 12 years
Years: 1952 to 2007
Show the code 
fig = px.scatter(
    df,
    x='gdpPercap',
    y='lifeExp',
    animation_frame='year',
    animation_group='country',
    size='pop',
    color='continent',
    hover_name='country',
    log_x=True,
    size_max=55,
    range_x=[100, 100000],
    range_y=[25, 90],
    title='Wealth vs Life Expectancy, 1952 to 2007',
    labels={
        'gdpPercap': 'GDP per Capita (USD, log scale)',
        'lifeExp': 'Life Expectancy (years)',
        'pop': 'Population'
    },
    template='plotly_white'
)
fig.update_layout(height=550)
fig.show()

This is the chart Hans Rosling made famous. The headline story is remarkable — across the period, every continent moved up and to the right. The world as a whole is healthier and wealthier than it was in 1952.

Life Expectancy Over Time

Show the code 
continent_avg = df.groupby(['year', 'continent'])['lifeExp'].mean().reset_index()

fig = px.line(
    continent_avg,
    x='year',
    y='lifeExp',
    color='continent',
    markers=True,
    title='Average Life Expectancy by Continent',
    labels={'lifeExp': 'Life Expectancy (years)', 'year': 'Year'},
    color_discrete_sequence=px.colors.qualitative.Set1,
    template='plotly_white'
)
fig.update_layout(height=450)
fig.update_traces(line=dict(width=3))
fig.show()

Africa’s trajectory is notably different — the curve flattens around the 1990s, reflecting the devastating impact of the HIV/AIDS epidemic on sub-Saharan countries. Asia shows the steepest gains of any continent over the period.

The Gains, Ranked

Show the code 
first_year = df[df['year'] == 1952][['country', 'continent', 'lifeExp']].rename(columns={'lifeExp': 'lifeExp_1952'})
last_year = df[df['year'] == 2007][['country', 'lifeExp']].rename(columns={'lifeExp': 'lifeExp_2007'})

change = first_year.merge(last_year, on='country')
change['gain'] = change['lifeExp_2007'] - change['lifeExp_1952']
top_gains = change.nlargest(15, 'gain').sort_values('gain')

fig = px.bar(
    top_gains,
    x='gain',
    y='country',
    color='continent',
    orientation='h',
    title='Top 15 Countries by Life Expectancy Gain (1952 → 2007)',
    labels={'gain': 'Years gained', 'country': ''},
    color_discrete_sequence=px.colors.qualitative.Bold,
    template='plotly_white'
)
fig.update_layout(height=500)
fig.show()

Oman leads the table — a country that transformed its healthcare infrastructure dramatically over this period. Several Asian countries feature heavily, reflecting the rapid development seen in that region.

The relationship is still clearly logarithmic — each additional unit of wealth buys less longevity than the last. A country at USD 1,000 per capita gains far more years from doubling to USD 2,000 than a country at USD 30,000 would from doubling to USD 60,000.

summary = df.groupby(['continent', 'year']).agg(

## Overview

This project uses the **Gapminder** dataset — the same data that Hans Rosling used in his famous TED talks[^1] — to explore how the world has changed between 1952 and 2007. The dataset contains life expectancy, GDP per capita, and population for 142 countries across 12 five-year snapshots.

[^1]: Rosling, H. (2006). *The best stats you've ever seen*. TED Talk. The Gapminder foundation maintains the underlying data.

::: {.callout-note}
## Questions this analysis answers
1. How has global life expectancy changed over time?
2. Is there still a strong link between wealth and longevity?
3. Which continents have gained the most?
:::

## The Relationship Between Wealth and Life Expectancy

```{python}
#| label: load-gapminder
#| code-summary: "Show setup code"

import plotly.express as px
import pandas as pd

df = px.data.gapminder()
print(f"Dataset covers {df['country'].nunique()} countries across {df['year'].nunique()} years")
print(f"Years: {df['year'].min()} to {df['year'].max()}")
```

```{python}
#| label: fig-animated-bubble
#| fig-cap: "Animated bubble chart: GDP per capita vs life expectancy over time. Press play to watch 55 years of progress."

fig = px.scatter(
    df,
    x='gdpPercap',
    y='lifeExp',
    animation_frame='year',
    animation_group='country',
    size='pop',
    color='continent',
    hover_name='country',
    log_x=True,
    size_max=55,
    range_x=[100, 100000],
    range_y=[25, 90],
    title='Wealth vs Life Expectancy, 1952 to 2007',
    labels={
        'gdpPercap': 'GDP per Capita (USD, log scale)',
        'lifeExp': 'Life Expectancy (years)',
        'pop': 'Population'
    },
    template='plotly_white'
)
fig.update_layout(height=550)
fig.show()
```

This is the chart Hans Rosling made famous. The headline story is remarkable — across the period, every continent moved **up and to the right**. The world as a whole is healthier and wealthier than it was in 1952.

## Life Expectancy Over Time

```{python}
#| label: fig-life-trend
#| fig-cap: "Continental average life expectancy over time"

continent_avg = df.groupby(['year', 'continent'])['lifeExp'].mean().reset_index()

fig = px.line(
    continent_avg,
    x='year',
    y='lifeExp',
    color='continent',
    markers=True,
    title='Average Life Expectancy by Continent',
    labels={'lifeExp': 'Life Expectancy (years)', 'year': 'Year'},
    color_discrete_sequence=px.colors.qualitative.Set1,
    template='plotly_white'
)
fig.update_layout(height=450)
fig.update_traces(line=dict(width=3))
fig.show()
```

Africa's trajectory is notably different — the curve flattens around the 1990s, reflecting the devastating impact of the HIV/AIDS epidemic on sub-Saharan countries. Asia shows the steepest gains of any continent over the period.

## The Gains, Ranked

```{python}
#| label: fig-gains
#| fig-cap: "Top 15 countries by absolute gain in life expectancy, 1952 to 2007"

first_year = df[df['year'] == 1952][['country', 'continent', 'lifeExp']].rename(columns={'lifeExp': 'lifeExp_1952'})
last_year = df[df['year'] == 2007][['country', 'lifeExp']].rename(columns={'lifeExp': 'lifeExp_2007'})

change = first_year.merge(last_year, on='country')
change['gain'] = change['lifeExp_2007'] - change['lifeExp_1952']
top_gains = change.nlargest(15, 'gain').sort_values('gain')

fig = px.bar(
    top_gains,
    x='gain',
    y='country',
    color='continent',
    orientation='h',
    title='Top 15 Countries by Life Expectancy Gain (1952 → 2007)',
    labels={'gain': 'Years gained', 'country': ''},
    color_discrete_sequence=px.colors.qualitative.Bold,
    template='plotly_white'
)
fig.update_layout(height=500)
fig.show()
```

Oman leads the table — a country that transformed its healthcare infrastructure dramatically over this period. Several Asian countries feature heavily, reflecting the rapid development seen in that region.

    trendline_color_override='gray'

The relationship is still clearly logarithmic — each additional unit of wealth buys less longevity than the last. A country at USD 1,000 per capita gains far more years from doubling to USD 2,000 than a country at USD 30,000 would from doubling to USD 60,000.

## Summary Statistics

```{python}
#| label: summary-table

summary = df.groupby(['continent', 'year']).agg(
    avg_life_exp=('lifeExp', 'mean'),
    avg_gdp=('gdpPercap', 'mean'),
    total_pop=('pop', 'sum')
).reset_index()

summary_wide = summary.pivot(index='continent', columns='year', values='avg_life_exp').round(1)
summary_wide[[1952, 1977, 2007]].style.background_gradient(cmap='RdYlGn', axis=None)
```
